📖 Plain-English guide — what every label on this dashboard means (tap to open)
The engines & tabs
TCE (Trend Convergence Engine) — the core stock-picker. It checks 9 separate signals on a stock and rates it HIGH / WATCH / IGNORE by how many line up (“converge”) at once.
Conviction — how many of the 5 strongest signals (the ones about the business actually improving) are firing. A US stock reaches HIGH only if at least 4 fire.
GUARD (relative-strength guardrail) — did the stock beat the overall US market (the SPY S&P-500 fund) over the last 6 months, and by how many points. If it’s losing to the market it “vetoes” a HIGH down to WATCH — a stock that can’t keep up with the market doesn’t earn top conviction. (“+183pt” = beat the market by 183 percentage points.)
EXPLOSIVE screen — a separate filter that flags young companies with fast, accelerating growth: tomorrow’s potential big winners, caught early.
Multibagger tabs — curated watchlists of names that could multiply in value; the engine’s job here is to confirm strength, not whether the stock is cheap today.
IM3 — the fundamental quality score, out of 162 points, graded A/B/C/FAIL. It answers “is this a financially sound business,” separately from price.
IG2 / Banking InvestoGenie 2.0 (“System B”) — banks can’t be judged like factories, so banks use this dedicated 24-ratio / 48-point bank model instead of IM3.
The chips on each row
V · G · M (Style) — Value (is it cheap?), Growth (growing & speeding up?), Momentum (is the market already moving on it?). Describes a stock’s style; never changes its rating. A Low Value (expensive-looking) is normal for an early winner — context, not a penalty.
Trend (vs 200-DMA) — ▲ Uptrend = price above its 200-day average (healthy); ▼ Downtrend = below it (falling-knife risk); golden / death = the 50-day average crossing above / below the 200-day (bullish / bearish shift). DMA = day moving average.
The 9 TCE signals: News = press coverage · Insider = company insiders buying their own stock (SEC filings) · Vol = today’s volume vs a normal day · Mom = recent price momentum · Margin = profit growing faster than sales (getting more profitable) · Capital = short interest (how many bet against it) · EPS-rev / Rev-rev = analysts raising or cutting forecasts · Target = upside to the average analyst price target · Rec = share of analysts saying “buy.”
▲FWD = analysts expect next year’s earnings to jump and/or just raised estimates · ⚡Z #1/#2 = the stock’s Zacks Rank (#1 Strong Buy, #2 Buy — the estimate-revision model).
Scores & valuation
IM3 grades: A ≥80% · B ≥60% · C ≥50% · FAIL <50% (banks: ELITE ≥85% · STRONG ≥75% · SOLID ≥60% · FAIR ≥50% · WEAK). IV = estimated fair price · MoS = margin of safety (how far below fair value the price is; positive = cheap) · PEG = P/E ÷ growth (under ~1 = growth is cheaply priced) · CAGR = compound annual growth rate · CFO = cash from operations · ROE = return on equity.
Markets & macro
PSX = Pakistan Stock Exchange · KSE-100 = its main index · SPY = the S&P 500 (US market benchmark) · SBP = State Bank of Pakistan (sets the policy rate) · REER = real effective exchange rate (is the rupee over/under-valued) · CPI / PCE = inflation gauges · DXY = US-dollar index · COT = futures positioning (who’s long/short) · GVZ = gold’s “fear gauge” · WALCL = the size of the US Fed’s balance sheet.
Tab 16 — World ETF Engine
UCITS = a European-regulated fund structure. As a UAE resident, buying Irish or Luxembourg-domiciled UCITS ETFs (rather than US-domiciled ones) avoids US estate-tax exposure above $60,000. Every fund on this tab is a UCITS ETF, buyable on IBKR.
The four doors — four independent ways the engine can recommend a fund: Country door (a country’s stock market beat the dollar over 6 months, ranked in the top 3) · Sector door (a US sector is scored Booming or Favoured) · Theme door (one of 4 global groupings — Asia-Tech, Developed-West, Commodity-Bloc, Emerging — is Booming or Favoured; Asia-Tech also triggers a dedicated semiconductor pick) · EM ex-China door (emerging markets overall are strong but China specifically is not, so a fund holding EM stocks minus China is recommended instead of a broad EM fund).
Double-confirmed (★) — the SAME fund was independently recommended by two different doors at once. This is not a stronger opinion from one signal — it is two separate calculations agreeing. Shown in plain text on each card (e.g. “ranked #1 country by return AND is the top country driving a Booming theme”).
Momentum-Watch — a separate list of the 55 non-leveraged UCITS funds already up 35% or more this year, plus the top 5 Nasdaq-100 trackers added on request despite sitting below that bar (real US mega-cap tech coverage, clearly marked as the exception they are). This is not the engine’s advice — it is an observation list of what is already running, so strong performers outside the engine’s own doors don’t go unseen.
Hydrogen Watch — 4 hydrogen-economy funds tracked separately because they were missing ISINs (fund ID codes) in the main data file and had to be sourced individually; shown as a distinct thematic list, not an engine recommendation.
Metals ETC Watch — gold, silver, platinum, palladium, and copper ETCs (Exchange-Traded Commodities — the metal itself, held in a vault, not a company; copper is the one synthetic/futures-based exception). Paired with the live trend, RSI, and (gold/silver/copper) COT positioning already shown on Tab 12 Metals.

Explosive Stocks (US + PSX) — Live Scanner Feed

📊 How to read the Signals (A · B) — what makes a stock EXPLOSIVE

Every stock is judged on two signals. The two dots in the Signals column show whether each one is found (● green = found) or missing (○ hollow = missing).

Signal A — Growth. The business is genuinely growing. It fires only when both are true on the latest annual financials: revenue up more than 20% year-over-year (chip G1) and operating profit up at least 15% (chip G2).

Signal B — Acceleration. Profit is speeding up, not just growing. It fires when operating-profit growth is over 20% and net profit is growing faster than operating profit — the net-profit ÷ operating-profit growth ratio is above 1.0 (chip A1), meaning gains compound as they flow to the bottom line. Guard: if last year’s operating profit was zero or negative, Signal B is held off (you can’t measure acceleration off a non-positive base).

How they combine into the verdict:
● + ● both → 🚀 EXPLOSIVE — growing and accelerating
● A only → 📈 QUALITY-GROWTH — growing, profit not yet accelerating
● B only → ⤴ INFLECTION — accelerating off a sub-20% top line (verify the turn)
○ + ○ neither → ✗ NOT EXPLOSIVE — or “OP declining” if operating profit is shrinking

The two dots map onto the detailed Conditions chips beside them: G1 + G2 = Signal A, A1 = Signal B. The other chips (A2 = pre-tax/operating ratio; C1/C3 = cash backing earnings) are extra context, not part of the two core signals. All figures are year-over-year from annual financial statements.

📊 CFTC CMX Non-Commercial Net Positions —
What this shows: how one-sided the big futures bets are. Each bar is the share of bets that are “long” (betting the price rises). Above ~20% means the crowd is heavily piled on one side, which tends to snap back — so read it as a crowding warning, not a buy/sell signal. COT Positioning Index — Stretch & Flip (9 Futures Contracts)
🧭 How stretched is positioning, and which way is it turning —
What this shows: the COT Index turns each contract’s speculator net position into a 0–100 score versus its own last year (1Y) and three years (3Y). Low (≤20) = crowded short / washed-out (often room to rally); high (≥80) = crowded long / extended (often room to fall); the middle is neutral. Flip is the net position as a % of open interest, and the 4w / 13w tags read BUY when the latest flip is above its recent average (positioning improving) or SELL when below. RSI is a momentum read on the net-position line (our own standard RSI, not a cloned proprietary one). Read this as positioning context, not a buy/sell signal on its own.
